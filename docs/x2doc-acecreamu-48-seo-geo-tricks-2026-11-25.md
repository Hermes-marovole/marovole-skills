### Slide 02 / 08 — The Free Backlinks（免费外链，8 条）
> 副标题：`High-DR profile links. Mostly nofollow: they get a new site found and trusted, not ranked overnight.`

1. A Chrome Web Store dev account is a one-time $5. That's a DR 100 link from Google.
2. Link your site from your GitHub profile and repo READMEs. DR 97.
3. Claim your company page on Crunchbase. DR 91. Fill every field.
4. List on G2 and Capterra. That's where buyers compare tools.
5. Launch on Product Hunt. DR 92 link, even if you don't hit #1.
6. Repost blogs on Medium, dev.to and Hashnode with a canonical link back.
7. Start a Substack for founder notes and link home from every post.
8. Fill about.me, Carrd and Linktree with one link: your site.

### Slide 03 / 08 — The Search Console Goldmine（GSC 挖词金矿，8 条）
> 副标题：`Google already shows you what people ask before they find you. Most founders never filter for it.`

1. Regex filter `^(?:\S+\s+){7,}\S+$` shows every query with 8+ words.
2. Set the date range to the last 16 months to catch more of them.
3. High impressions + low clicks = a question you rank for but don't answer.
4. Answer it in the first lines of the page that already ranks.
5. Filter queries by how / what / why to get real questions, word for word.
6. Fix pages stuck on positions 8–15 first. They're one push from page one.
7. Filter by your brand name and watch branded searches after each launch.
8. Run URL Inspection → Request indexing on every new page.

### Slide 04 / 08 — The Borrowed Authority（借权寄生，8 条）
> 副标题：`Publish on domains Google already trusts. Rankings come in hours, but they're rented, so point them home.`

1. Publish a LinkedIn article. One live test hit #1 on Google in 38 minutes.
2. Feed the AI a 30-min founder interview transcript for that article.
3. Cover one exact keyword on 7 platforms in a week. One test got 7 page-one spots.
4. Test pages on a GitHub Gist, a NotebookLM link or Google Apps Script.
5. Expect most to drop out of the index. Borrowed authority fades.
6. Take the top Reddit thread for your keyword. Rewrite it better on your site.
7. Start your own subreddit and post the same answer there.
8. Always link back. Mentions from strong sources are what stays.

### Slide 05 / 08 — The Reddit Playbook（Reddit 运营手册，8 条）
> 副标题：`Reddit threads rank on Google and get quoted in AI answers. Make the account ban-proof first.`

1. Google `site:reddit.com` + your problem to find threads that already rank.
2. Pick subreddits with 10k–50k+ members where your buyers compare tools.
3. Read the rules. Some only allow self-promo in a weekly thread.
4. Warm up for 1–3 weeks. Get to 30–50 karma before any mention.
5. Write 4–5 helpful comments for every product mention.
6. Say "I built this, so I'm biased" instead of hiding the plug.
7. Leave the link out. Put it in your profile.
8. Sort by Top → All time and copy the formats that already won.

### Slide 06 / 08 — Get Cited by ChatGPT（让 ChatGPT 引用你，8 条）
> 副标题：`If ChatGPT can't read your site, it recommends someone it can. And you'll never know.`

1. Add `llms.txt` to your site root: a markdown map of what you do.
2. Anthropic ships one. Copy the structure of `platform.claude.com/llms.txt`.
3. Check what GPTBot sees. Pages that render only in JS can look empty.
4. Make sure `robots.txt` doesn't block AI crawlers by accident.
5. Rewrite your About page: who you are, what you do, for whom. Plain words.
6. Pick one AI Overview and track which sources it cites for a week.
7. Write a page that covers those patterns better than the cited sources.
8. Track who ChatGPT names for your category, not just your Google rank.

### Slide 07 / 08 — Spy on Competitors（反查竞品漏斗与排名，8 条）
> 副标题：`What they track and what they rank for is public. You just have to know where to look.`

1. Find their `GTM-XXXXXX` ID in the page source.
2. Open `googletagmanager.com/gtm.js?id=` + that ID in your browser.
3. Ctrl+F `_awct` to see every Google Ads conversion they track.
4. The conversion names are their funnel: Registration → Trial → First Payment.
5. Pull their commercial keywords. Skip SERPs full of parasite pages.
6. Match the format of the #1 result: listicle, tool or guide.
7. Add internal links the day you publish, not later.
8. Recheck rankings every week and update whatever slips.

### Slide 08 / 08 — The one that matters most now（收尾与商业导流）

  > The best SEO trick isn't on any list. Open ChatGPT and ask: "What's the best tool for my category?" If it names your competitor, it's pulling from pages that mention them, not you. So find the pages it cites and get on them. (Listicles, reviews, Reddit threads. Not your own guess.)

## 四、官方文档一手核查摘录（2026-11-25 实查）

### 核查 1：OpenAI 官方爬虫分工（`OAI-SearchBot` vs `GPTBot` vs `ChatGPT-User`）
- 来源：https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots
- 原文摘录：
  > "OpenAI uses OAI-SearchBot and GPTBot robots.txt tags to enable webmasters to manage how their sites and content work with AI. Each setting is independent of the others – for example, a webmaster can allow OAI-SearchBot in order to appear in search results while disallowing GPTBot to indicate that crawled content should not be used for training OpenAI’s generative AI foundation models."
  > "For search results, please note it can take ~24 hours from a site’s robots.txt update for our systems to adjust."
  > "OAI-SearchBot is for search. OAI-SearchBot is used to surface websites in search results in ChatGPT’s search features. Sites that are opted out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers, though can still appear as navigational links."
  > "GPTBot is used to make our generative AI foundation models more useful and safe. It is used to crawl content that may be used in training our generative AI foundation models."
  > "ChatGPT-User is not used to determine whether content may appear in Search. Please use OAI-SearchBot in robots.txt for managing Search opt outs and automatic crawl."

### 核查 2：Google Search 官方指南对 `llms.txt` 与「不真实提及」的表态
- 来源：https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide （页脚标注 `Last updated 2026-07-10 UTC.`）
- 原文摘录：
  > "LLMS.txt files and other \"special\" markup: You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, markup, or Markdown to appear in Google Search (including its generative AI capabilities), as Google Search itself doesn't use them."
  > "Seeking inauthentic \"mentions\": Just like the rest of Google Search, our generative AI features can show what's being said about products and services across the web, including in blogs, videos, and forum discussions. However, seeking inauthentic \"mentions\" across the web isn't as helpful as it might seem."
- 补充来源（Chrome Lighthouse 官方文档）：https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/agentic-browsing/llms-txt （页脚标注 `Last updated 2026-05-05 UTC.`）
  > "The `llms.txt` file is an emerging convention used to provide a machine-readable summary of a website's content, specifically designed for LLMs and AI agents. Without this file, agents may spend more time crawling the site to understand its high-level structure and primary content."
  > "If the file is not provided by the server (resulting in a 404), the audit is marked as Not Applicable (N/A), as providing the file is optional at the moment."
- 实测 `https://platform.claude.com/llms.txt`：HTTP 200，返回 `text/plain; charset=utf-8`，开头为 `# Anthropic Developer Documentation`，列出文档总览、MCP server 地址与 SDK 版本 JSON 入口。

### 核查 3：Google 网站声誉滥用政策（Site Reputation Abuse）
- 来源：https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/11/site-reputation-abuse （发布日期：`Tuesday, November 19, 2024`）
- 原文摘录：
  > "Site reputation abuse is the practice of publishing third-party pages on a site in an attempt to abuse search rankings by taking advantage of the host site's ranking signals."
  > "We're making it clear that using third-party content on a site in an attempt to exploit the site's ranking signals is a violation of this policy — regardless of whether there is first-party involvement or oversight of the content."
  > "Aside from site reputation abuse issues, we also have systems and methods designed to understand if a section of a site is independent or starkly different from the main content of the site. By treating these areas as if they are standalone sites, it better ensures a level playing field, so that sub-sections of sites don't get a ranking boost just because of the reputation of the main site."
